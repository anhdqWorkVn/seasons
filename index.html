Four Seasons of My Childhood

Spring

I spent my childhood in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam, and I remember being around 14 years old during the time I am describing. Spring in Ho Chi Minh City was very different from spring in places with four clear seasons. The weather was usually warm and sunny, and the temperature was often around the 80s Fahrenheit. There was not a major change from cold weather to warm weather because the city has a tropical climate.

What made spring special to me was Tet, the Vietnamese Lunar New Year. My family cleaned and decorated the house, prepared food, and spent time visiting relatives. The streets were colorful with flowers and decorations. For me, spring was less about a change in temperature and more about family, celebration, and the feeling of starting a new year.

Summer

Summer in Ho Chi Minh City was hot and humid. Temperatures during the day could reach around 90 degrees Fahrenheit or higher. When I was around 14, summer also meant having more free time because school was on break. I remember spending more time at home, going out with family and friends, and trying to stay cool during the hottest parts of the day.

Summer was also connected with the rainy season. Heavy rain could suddenly begin even when the day had been hot and sunny. Sometimes there were thunderstorms with strong rain that flooded some streets for a short period. These storms were a normal part of life in the city, and people usually waited for the rain to become lighter before continuing with their day.

Fall

Fall in Ho Chi Minh City did not have colorful leaves and cool temperatures like the fall season in some other countries. The weather was still warm, and rain was common. When I was 14, this time of year usually meant returning to school after summer vacation. My daily routine became busier with classes, homework, and spending time with classmates.

Rain was one of the things I remember most during this period. Some afternoons had heavy showers, and traveling around the city could become more difficult. Even though the weather did not look like a traditional fall season, I still associated this part of the year with a change in my routine. Summer vacation was over, and a new school year had started.

Winter

Winter in Ho Chi Minh City was nothing like the winters I later learned about in colder climates. There was no snow, and I did not need a heavy winter coat. The weather was generally warm, although some mornings and evenings could feel a little cooler and more comfortable. There was also usually less rain than during the rainy months.

As a teenager, I enjoyed this time of year because the weather made it easier to spend time outside. Toward the end of the year, the city also became busier with holiday decorations and preparations for the upcoming New Year and Tet season. Looking back, winter in my childhood reminds me of comfortable weather, family activities, and the excitement of another year coming to an end.
